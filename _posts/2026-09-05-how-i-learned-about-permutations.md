I didn't write for a while because I was finishing one of the papers that suppose to form my PhD thesis. It definitely doesn't mean I'm done with all the projects left for me to do at this position. But since I was working on this paper I was learning a new analysis method for me - permutations. And since it was something new and something I'd have to understand from the scratch, I decided to share here the resources that helped me to do so as well as the code I wrote for this paper where we analyzed pupil data. The analysis script is adapted from my supervisor's pipeline from her postdoc times (thank GOD it existed), which itself builds on two helper functions - eye_glm (Tobias Hauser) and ols (Tim Behrens). Probably it's not super logical to make a second post about this since I wanted to bring up resources that could be useful for a researcher at the beginning of their journey, but I thought that this would be at least up to date with my life.

I think this code could be helpful for one who wants to build an understanding of how trial-wise time course analysis works in pupil research, and for ones who want to understand how permutations work because I think this approach can be adapted to a lot of different data at the end of the day - as long as you're careful about what you're allowed to shuffle. Unfortunately, I cannot share the raw data here but just fyi these are from EyeLink, already cleaned from blinks and interpolated before going into this script (we use a FieldTrip MATLAB package to work with them). Also here are the links that I found useful: [video 1](https://youtu.be/FkHkB-IadxY?si=jVB-keifEwgZwMfM) and [video 2](https://youtu.be/zg0tmnGGwBI?si=2zo9J-MeTEcobjtB) from the [mumfordbrainstats](https://www.youtube.com/@mumfordbrainstats) YouTube channel, and [my GitHub repo](https://github.com/AlinaZhunussova/pupillometry_GLM) for the code.
